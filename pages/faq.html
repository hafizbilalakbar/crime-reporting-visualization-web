Common Questions

Everything you need to know about the CrimeWatch platform

What is CrimeWatch?

CrimeWatch is a community-focused platform that allows citizens to report crime incidents, visualize crime patterns through interactive maps, and helps communities and law enforcement collaborate for safer neighborhoods.

Is CrimeWatch available in my area?

CrimeWatch is currently available in over 120 communities nationwide. To check if your area is covered, please enter your ZIP code or city name in the search box on our homepage.

Do I need to create an account to use CrimeWatch?

No account is required to view crime maps and general information. However, creating a free account allows you to submit reports, receive updates on your reported incidents, and customize your map preferences.

Is CrimeWatch a replacement for emergency services?

No. CrimeWatch is not an emergency response service. For immediate emergencies, always call 911 or your local emergency number first. CrimeWatch is designed for non-emergency reporting and crime visualization.

How do I report a crime on CrimeWatch?

To report a crime, click on the "Report Crime" button in the navigation menu. Fill out the reporting form with as much detail as possible, including location, time, and description of the incident. You can also upload photos or videos as evidence.

Can I report anonymously?

Yes, you can choose to submit reports anonymously. However, providing contact information allows authorities to follow up if they need additional information to investigate the incident.

What happens after I submit a report?

Once submitted, your report is reviewed by authorized personnel for verification. You'll receive a reference number to track your report status. After verification, the report may be added to the crime map and shared with relevant authorities.

What types of evidence can I submit?

You can upload photos, videos, and audio recordings as evidence with your report. All file formats are supported up to 20MB per file. Please ensure your evidence does not violate anyone's privacy and is directly related to the incident.

How do I use the crime map?

Visit the "Crime Map" section to view the interactive map. You can filter by crime type, date range, and zoom in/out to focus on specific areas. Click on pins to view details about each incident, or switch to heatmap view to identify crime hotspots.

How often is the map updated?

The crime map is updated daily. New verified reports are added to the map within 24 hours of verification. Historical data is maintained for up to one year for pattern analysis.

What do the different colors and icons mean on the map?

Different icons represent various crime types (theft, vandalism, assault, etc.). In heatmap view, colors indicate density of incidents: blue for low density, green/yellow for medium, and red for high concentration areas.

Can I download or share map data?

Yes, you can download crime data in CSV format or share a snapshot of the map via social media or email. Look for the export and share buttons in the top right corner of the map interface.

How is my personal information protected?

We take data protection seriously. All personal information is encrypted and stored securely. We never share your contact details publicly. For anonymous reports, no personal information is collected. See our Privacy Policy for more details.

Who has access to the reports I submit?

Only authorized personnel and relevant law enforcement agencies have access to complete report details. Public map views show general information about incidents but exclude personal details of reporters or exact addresses for privacy reasons.

How long is my report data stored?

Report data is stored for up to two years for analysis and pattern recognition purposes. Personal information can be removed upon request after the initial investigation period (typically 90 days).

How do you prevent false reports?

All reports undergo a verification process by trained staff before being published on the map. Reports lacking sufficient detail or showing signs of being false are flagged for further review or rejected.
